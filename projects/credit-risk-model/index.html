Mortgage Default Risk: Pipeline and Model Lab

Predicts which Fannie Mae mortgages reach 90+ days past due within the next 12 months. A DuckDB pipeline builds 737 variables from 26.6 million monthly loan records, seven models are compared on a time-based split, and a second repo logs 35 trials to see what actually improves the model.

Architecture

Bronze, Silver, Gold, then models

StepWhat happens
BronzeA quarterly Fannie Mae file is streamed straight from its zip into Parquet. All 114 columns stay as text.
SilverColumns are typed. An unknown delinquency status becomes NULL instead of a made-up number.
GoldOne row per loan and observation month. 737 variables from SQL window functions, plus a label for 90+ days late in the next 12 months.
ModelsAn 18-variable scorecard, logistic regression, decision tree, random forest, XGBoost, LightGBM and a neural network, all on one split.
EvaluationAUC, KS, lift, calibration, SHAP, population stability and a cost-based cut-off table.

The model lab: what actually improves a model

What the real data taught me

Findings I did not expect

  • Variables at month t read only rows at or before t, and the label reads only rows after t. Tests change later months and check that nothing moves.
  • The split is by calendar time with 12-month gaps, not a random 80/20. Each loan appears many times, so a random split would put the same loan in training and test.
  • Boosted models score about 0.92 AUC on the test period, but among borrowers with no late month in 24 months a simple scorecard ranks as well or better (0.761 against 0.741). Most of the headline gap comes from loans that are already late.
  • In the model lab, training on all training loans was the only change that beat seed noise (0.923 to 0.930 test AUC). Tuning, fewer variables and monotone constraints stayed within about 0.001.
  • Weighting defaults three times and isotonic calibration both made log loss worse, so I left them out.
  • The default rate fell from 3.6% in training to 1.8% in the test period, so any cut-off needs re-checking before real use.
  • The 2026 files have 114 columns but the public layout documents 110. The extra four are named and left unused.
